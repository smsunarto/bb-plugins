# Unslop

Edit the supplied or in-scope prose for clarity. Preserve facts, uncertainty, voice, and
useful detail. These patterns are diagnostic cues, not banned tokens. Keep a technical
term, quotation, or deliberate stylistic choice when it serves the reader.

Rule numbers are stable identifiers that other skills cite. Removed rules leave gaps.
Do not renumber them or expand this catalog during an unrelated writing task.

## Content

3. **Unsupported participial claims.** Phrases such as "highlighting" or "ensuring" can
   append an unsupported conclusion. State the actual relationship or remove the claim.
4. **Vague attribution.** Name the source behind "experts believe" or "reports suggest".
   If evidence is unavailable, identify the uncertainty instead of inventing attribution.

## Language

7. **Inflated vocabulary.** Replace words such as "delve", "pivotal", or "showcase" when
   they add emphasis without meaning. Preserve precise technical usage.
8. **Indirect verbs.** Prefer "is" or "has" over "serves as" or "boasts" when the meaning
   is the same.
9. **Formulaic contrast.** Replace "not just X, but Y" with the actual point unless the
   contrast is needed to correct a likely misunderstanding.
10. **Forced groups of three.** Use the number of examples or options the subject needs.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Use one name for one thing instead of changing terms for variety.
12. **False ranges.** Use "from X to Y" only for a meaningful range. Otherwise name the topics.

## Style

13. **Dash-heavy prose.** Prefer periods or commas to em dashes. Preserve literal syntax
    and quoted material rather than applying a blind punctuation replacement.
14. **Colon-heavy prose.** Use colons for useful introductions, lists, or examples. Rewrite
    a sentence when the colon merely substitutes for a clear grammatical relationship.
15. **Excessive bold.** Emphasize what readers need to scan, not every name or acronym.
16. **Redundant inline headings.** A label such as "Performance: Performance improved"
    repeats itself. Keep a lead-in when it identifies the item and introduces new detail.
17. **Title-case headings.** Prefer sentence case unless the document has another convention.
18. **Decorative emojis.** Remove decoration that adds no meaning. Retain requested status
    indicators or product conventions.
19. **Curly quotes.** Prefer straight quotes in source-oriented prose. Preserve quotations
    and typography required by the deliverable.

## Communication artifacts

20. **Chatbot filler.** Remove stock openings and endings such as "Of course" or "I hope
    this helps" when they add no task-relevant information.
21. **Sycophancy.** Replace automatic praise or agreement with the supported answer.

## Filler

23. **Empty phrases.** "In order to" can be "to". Delete "it is important to note" unless
    the importance itself needs an explanation.
24. **Stacked hedging.** Replace "could potentially possibly" with the uncertainty actually
    supported by the evidence. Do not remove necessary qualification.
25. **Generic conclusions.** End with a specific result or next action when needed.
    Omit a conclusion that only restates the response.

## Jargon

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** Words such as "substrate", "nexus", "north star", or
    "flywheel" can conceal the mechanism. Name the actual owner, operation, or constraint.
    Keep established terms such as "test harness" or "API surface" when they are precise
    for the audience. Do not invent a plainer synonym that changes the meaning.

## Plain speech

27. **Unspecified benefits.** Replace "types that follow your schema" with the actual
    mechanism or result when it matters, such as "a column rename fails the build".
    A general sentence can still be useful context. Specificity is not a requirement to
    invent a measurement or project detail.
28. **Dense sentences.** Split competing ideas or clarify their relationship. Keep a
    condition with its action when separating them would make the instruction harder to use.
29. **Hidden actors.** Prefer active voice when naming the actor clarifies responsibility.
    Passive voice is suitable when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
30. **Unmeasured intensifiers.** Replace "significantly improves" with supported evidence
    or a narrower claim. Keep adverbs that express a real timing or degree distinction.
31. **Needlessly formal words.** Prefer "use" to "utilize" and "if" to "in the event that"
    when precision is unchanged.
32. **Mannered phrasing.** Replace aphorisms, personified code, and rhetorical fragments
    with the action or fact they mean. Rule 26 covers metaphor nouns.
33. **Over-compression.** Restore articles, verbs, and useful connective words when fragments,
    abbreviations, or arrows make prose hard to parse. Compact notation remains useful in
    a diagram, table, or code example.
